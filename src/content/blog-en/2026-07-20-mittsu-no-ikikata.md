Depending on where you place the conversion of your time, there are three ways to live.

Accumulating it outside yourself. Things you've built, systems, trust. These keep moving even if you stop, and they benefit from compound interest. In exchange, you shoulder the heaviest risk: it doesn't turn into income until it solidifies.

Accumulating it inside yourself. Skills that work anywhere. The ability to carry them with you becomes your insurance—being employable anywhere means being able to leave anywhere. However, because it requires your own time every time you want to withdraw value, your working hours become the ceiling for your income.

Not accumulating at all. In exchange for letting go of compound interest, you receive the full value of the present. You exercise sovereignty over your time right now. Freelancers and part-timers are legitimate residents of this path, and that is also fine.

All three are correct. There is only one kind of failure—a mismatch between the path you are on and the reward you expect. The worst mismatch is "accumulating in someone else's vessel." This means pouring years into skills that only work within a specific company. Since it isn't inside you, you can't take it with you; and since it isn't your own thing outside yourself, you don't own it.

There is no such thing as losing on any of these paths. There is only the mistake of misidentifying which path you are on.
