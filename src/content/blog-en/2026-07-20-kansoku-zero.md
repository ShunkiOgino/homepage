Job hunting is a system that forces people who have never worked—in other words, people who have never once observed their own output—to make one of the biggest decisions of their lives. It is fundamentally flawed, akin to being told to fix a malfunction in a machine that has no logs. That is why everyone answers based on desire, and because they answer with desire, they miss the mark.

For a moment, I want you to recall when you decided on your current company (or the one that gave you an offer). You likely researched the company using facts. Annual salary, overtime hours, reviews—you probably gathered a full set of numbers and testimonies. However, when it comes to yourself, the materials at hand suddenly become flimsy. A vague impression that "I might like talking to people," four letters from a personality test, and a "student achievements" story polished for interviews. While one side is filled with facts, the other is filled with desires and fabrications.

![While I researched the company's salary, overtime, and reviews with facts, my own self-assessment consisted of "I might like talking," four letters from a personality test, and a fabricated story about student achievements](/img/pipes/kansoku-zero.png)

You chose the company based on facts, but you chose your own fit based on subjectivity—most job-hunting deals are struck upon this asymmetry. While you meticulously research the party you are buying from, you declare your own value—the side offering years of your time—without having properly observed yourself. This is, in many cases, the true identity of what we call a "mismatch."

Therefore, the reason job hunting isn't going well is not because you lack self-analysis. It is because the materials (records) to analyze were structurally non-existent. This is not your negligence. The order is simply reversed—you cannot think and then work; you can only think based on the records of having worked.

And for those who have experience working, you already have hundreds of times more material at your fingertips than you did back then. What was sustainable, and what wasn't. What you grew accustomed to, and what you couldn't get used to. These are all facts that have already occurred, waiting to be read. You simply haven't read them yet.
