Job hunting is a system that forces people who have never worked—in other words, people who have never once observed their own output—to make one of the biggest decisions of their lives. It is fundamentally flawed, akin to being told to fix a malfunction in a machine that has no logs. That is why everyone answers based on desire, and because they answer based on desire, they miss the mark.

For a moment, try to recall when you decided on your current company (or the one that gave you an offer). You likely researched the company using facts. You looked up the annual salary. You checked the overtime hours. You even read the reviews. But what about yourself? "I might like talking to people." Four letters from a personality test. Your "student achievements" were essentially an essay.

![While the company was researched via facts like salary, overtime, and reviews, self-knowledge consisted of "I might like talking," four letters from a personality test, and an essay for student achievements](/img/pipes/kansoku-zero.png)

You chose the company based on facts, yet didn't you choose your own fit based purely on subjectivity? You investigated only one side of the transaction, while for the other side—the self that would be handing over several years of time—you declared based on desires and impressions. This is the true identity of what we call a "mismatch."

Therefore, the reason job hunting doesn't go well is not because of a lack of self-analysis. It is because the materials for analysis (the records) were structurally non-existent. This is not your negligence. The order is simply reversed—you cannot think and then work; you can only think based on the records of having worked.

And for those who have already worked, you already have hundreds of times more material at your fingertips than you did back then. What lasted and what didn't. What you grew accustomed to and what you couldn't get used to. These are all facts that have already occurred, waiting to be read. You simply haven't read them yet.
