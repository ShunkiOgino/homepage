Job hunting is a system that forces people who have never worked—in other words, people who have never once observed their own output—to make one of the biggest decisions of their lives. It is fundamentally impossible, akin to being told to fix a malfunction in a machine that has no logs. That is why everyone answers based on desire, and because they answer with desire, they miss the mark.

As an experiment, try counting the amount of time you actually spent "looking inside" when you chose your current company (or the one you received an offer from). Information sessions, interviews, alumni visits. Even if you add it all up, it was probably only a few to a dozen hours at most. You were forced to decide where to spend several years of your life based on that amount of time. If you think of it as a purchase, it is an abnormal transaction.

![The time spent looking inside was only a few hours. What was decided is the next several years](/img/pipes/kansoku-zero.png)

The term "mismatch" is also a deception. Job postings only list the salary and promises (growth, fulfillment); they don't write a single line about the actual product—what you will actually see inside, or how many years it takes to learn everything.
A lottery ticket that doesn't define what a "win" is is destined from the start to end in disappointment. A failure isn't a problem with your discernment; it's that there was nothing to discern presented to you in the first place.

Therefore, the reason job hunting doesn't go well isn't because of a lack of self-analysis. It is because the materials for analysis (the records) were structurally nonexistent. It is not your negligence. The order is simply reversed—you cannot think and then work; you can only think based on the records of having worked.

And for those who have worked, they already have hundreds of times more material at their fingertips than they did back then. What lasted and what didn't. What they grew accustomed to and what they couldn't get used to. These are all facts that have already occurred, waiting to be read. You just haven't read them yet.
