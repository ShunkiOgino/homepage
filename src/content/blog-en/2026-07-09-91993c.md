Using a local LLM (Gemma), I automatically generate semantic links—along with the reasoning for why they connect—from all nodes of articles and works, storing them in JSON format. This creates a structure where one can jump directly from an article about "compound interest" to a work that embodies that philosophy, such as the "Video Studio," following the lines of meaning.

When organizing information, we tend to rely on the chronological constraint of "dates." However, the essence of thought should not be a linear accumulation, but rather an expansion like a network. Value is created the moment a fragmentary realization from one day connects with another insight from the past. I wanted to implement this "cross-sectional connectivity" as a system.

To achieve this, I modeled the structure of my thoughts like a Unix file system. The design allows "containment (themes)" via folders and "proximity (cross-sectional connections)" via tags to coexist as separate layers. In terms of experience, I intentionally avoid presenting the overall map, keeping only the local view currently open visible. By leaving the entire disk unknown and tracing thoughts through forward and backward navigation, I believe this incomplete field of vision fosters an exploratory thinking experience.

Gemma is what specifically constructed this network. This has made transitions possible based on "semantic adjacency" rather than mere hyperlinks.

Furthermore, I have linked this mechanism to the centralized management of "insight cards" (fragmentary knowledge in Markdown format). Once high-quality insights are accumulated, they can become blog posts and simultaneously be repurposed as knowledge sources for streaming characters. Considering the context limits of LLMs, I limit the reference range using tags (decks) to maintain response accuracy. In doing so, I am pursuing the "compound interest of content," where a single insight generates value through multiple outlets.

The value lies not in organizing and saving information, but in circulating it as a network of meaning and gaining new perspectives from unexpected connections. By designing the structure and introducing an LLM as a dynamic link, thought transforms from a mere record into a living asset.

The vast amount of notes and records you hold right now—should they be organized by a timeline, or are they nodes waiting to be connected by lines of meaning?
