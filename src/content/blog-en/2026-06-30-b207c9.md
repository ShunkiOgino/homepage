When you try to release only finished products, you run out of material.

Normally, the thinking goes like this: if you're going to release a work, you should polish it thoroughly and put it out into the world in its best, completed state. That is considered courtesy to the reader and the proper way to provide value.

However, with that design, the longer the production cycle of a work, the more likely you are to run out of fuel. While waiting for completion, communication stops and enthusiasm cools down. Even with the will to provide value, you are bound by the irreversible time constraint of production costs, and as a result, the frequency of your output drops. This is a structural problem.

Specifically, I tackled this by automating the production of demo videos. Eliminating the cost of traditional manual recording, I built a pipeline using Playwright to automate everything from UI operations to video output. By controlling transitions and subtitles with JSON-formatted scripts and introducing coordinate-based clicking for SVG nodes where selector specification is difficult, I have established a system that can generate demo videos rapidly even immediately after environment setup.

Previously, recording and editing took over an hour. The process of "making a video" became a bottleneck; even after feature implementation was finished, there were periods where I couldn't release it. However, by introducing this structure, the time has been reduced to a few minutes with a single command. I can now supply work-in-progress (WIP) behavior as content itself, creating a mechanism where the production cost itself is converted into output volume.

What these things have in common is placing value not on the "point of completion," but on the "line (process) of generation." By disclosing the production process like a "factory tour," I can turn the current state of "being in the middle of making it" into an asset, rather than forcing myself to squeeze out a final deliverable.

Is it really true that something has no value simply because it is unfinished?
