Heat, once escaped, never returns on its own. I wondered if I could turn this brutally simple law of physics into an experience that is not "read," but "felt through touch."

Normally, if you want to convey knowledge, you explain it carefully, provide diagrams, and present the correct answer. That is considered the educational and kind approach. But I want to do the exact opposite. Before teaching the logic, I want to force the "sensation of irreversibility" upon the body. I decided to confine the principles of thermodynamics—which make people sleepy when explained in words—within the form of a restrictive game.

Specifically, I designed a mechanism to move "points," which are small lights on the screen. In this world, points cannot generate heat on their own; if left alone, they gradually cool down and eventually all vanish.

At first, there was no motive to aggregate, and the points simply scattered. To that, I added a constraint called "surplus cost," where a point cannot move unless it maintains a certain amount of heat. When I adjusted the threshold for the minimum heat required for survival, "huddling together to gain warmth" became a rational choice for survival. This is the design where choices are stocked and accumulate, forming the core of compound interest.

From here, survival strategies split into two directions.

One is for points to collide and stick together. That momentum turns into heat, allowing them to warm up. However, in exchange for gaining warmth by huddling, their freedom of movement is restricted. This is a "static aggregation" for survival, a structure akin to a kind of redistribution.

The other is to consume stored heat and launch oneself with a targeted "committed ejection operation." Heat turns into motion, allowing the point to fly far. By making it an irrevocable design where the trajectory cannot be changed once launched, I gave physical weight to the decision. However, the heat spent on the shot never returns. To move is to spend heat—an irreversible dissipation.

Here, an ironic reversal occurs. The points that enjoy warmth and stabilize through aggregation lose the heat needed to move, and thus vanish first, unable to change. In contrast, only the points that dare to leap out in solitude can overcome the S-curve wall of difficulty and reach the next survival zone.

Huddle if you want warmth; spend heat if you want to move. Once launched, you cannot steer; you can only surrender yourself to gravity and momentum. Do you make the "irreversible move," or do you conserve heat and remain stationary? Through this balancing act, I aimed to create an intuitive experience of "why things cool down" and "how they can be maintained."

The moment you fire, you are personally eliminating the possibility of delivering warmth to a partner. This irreversible movement is the essence.

The true identity of this mechanism is the physical logic of the "Second Law of Thermodynamics" and "dissipative structures." Gain warmth, lose freedom; move, lose heat. This antinomy cannot be resolved by individual will or ethics; it is determined by the structure of physical cost.

"Static aggregation" for survival, and "dynamic dissipation" for evolution. While carrying this irreversible trade-off structure, how do you continue to maintain a fleeting form in a cooling world? I wanted people to experience that structure through the operation of their fingertips.

The mechanism is simple, but how it unfolds is unpredictable. I believe that this gap—where you "understand it, yet cannot predict it"—is what creates the interest that makes one want to keep playing.

Is the warmth you hold in your hand right now the price paid for surrendering your freedom?
