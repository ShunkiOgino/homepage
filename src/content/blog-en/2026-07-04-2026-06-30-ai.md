After switching the LLM from a 31B to Gemma-4's 12B model, initial latency dropped from about 5–6 seconds to 1.0–1.7 seconds. In environments requiring real-time responsiveness, such as live streaming, this difference of a few seconds is not just a number; it defines the user experience itself—the "tempo of conversation."

Generally, when running AI locally, one might think it's best to keep high-performance models ready in storage and resident in memory for immediate use. However, forcing this in an environment with memory limits leads to resource contention, and the system easily crashes. It is a contradiction: sacrificing stability in the pursuit of performance.

Therefore, I switched my design to one based on "exclusive use" and "on-demand acquisition" of resources.

First, to prevent crashes caused by memory shortages, I introduced a mutual exclusion mechanism. By ensuring resources are secured only while a specific model is in use and controlling the system so other models do not interfere, I have guaranteed operational stability.

I also abandoned the habit of "hoarding" storage. With a connection exceeding 100Mbps, even a model around 30GB can be re-acquired in 30 to 40 minutes. Rather than keeping everything locally and degrading performance, an operational approach of re-downloading as needed creates both physical and mental breathing room. My philosophy is to protect only "irreplaceable assets," such as custom fine-tuned models, and treat everything else as external storage.

Furthermore, I eliminated the GUI to move toward operational automation. Tools like OBS are convenient for humans, but they aren't suited for autonomous operation by AI. Instead, I adopted a script-based configuration: rendering Live2D via headless Chromium, combining the canvas and audio, and streaming to RTMP via ffmpeg. This has realized an environment that can be launched and controlled with a single command: `python broadcast.py`.

What emerges from this design is a perspective that treats the tradeoff between accuracy and speed as a structural question of "which one to choose at what timing." Instead of demanding maximum performance for every task, I optimize model size and resource allocation according to the objective. I believe this separation of concerns is the optimal solution for running AI practically on limited local resources.

Are you perhaps currently unable to move because you are trying to stack as many resources as possible? It is likely worth letting go of the need to "hoard" and considering whether the problem can be solved through structure.
