"[shrugs]" or "[nods deeply]." By having the AI output these kinds of gesture tags directly mixed into the script, I started to get a sense that the AI character was speaking of its own volition.

Until now, I had been building this using a post-hoc mapping structure: "Create script → Generate impression → Determine emotion → Apply motion."

To resolve this sense of unnaturalness, I fundamentally changed the motion control. I had the gesture tags output simultaneously during script generation, synchronizing the choice of words with the choice of acting. In other words, I switched to a design where the decision "If these words are chosen, they must be accompanied by this gesture" is made in a single stroke.

What is crucial here is not to think of "what to say" and "how to move" as separate entities, but to synchronize them as a single decision. A character's "... (pause)" in the script is output simultaneously as a concrete physical behavior, such as "averts gaze and shrugs." This shift in structure is the key to eliminating physical dissonance.

Are you controlling a reaction? Or a will?
