In the world of physics, there are concepts like the "Second Law of Thermodynamics" and "dissipative structures." Simply put, these theories suggest that once heat disperses, it never returns, and it is precisely within that irreversible flow that temporary order and form emerge. However, even when explained through words or formulas, these concepts are difficult for most people to understand intuitively. That is why I created *game_orbit*, a work that translates these laws into a game—an experience designed for "understanding through touch rather than reading."

At the core of this game is the management of "heat" as a resource. To move, the player must consume heat, and once heat is released, it never returns. By making every single operation an irreversible choice, the player comes to understand the "irreversibility of heat" not as a theory, but as a physical sensation.

Within the game, I designed a dilemma: "gather together if you want to stay warm, but move apart if you want to act." When particles collide and bond, heat is gained, but mobility is lost. Furthermore, I structured it so that accumulating bonded allies as a "core" becomes an asset, but the larger the core grows, the higher the maintenance cost becomes and the easier it is for the whole system to cool down. Since greedily expanding your scale leads to self-destruction, a strategy of cautious accumulation—always leaving some breathing room—is required.

The condition for leveling up is met only when both heat and movement are sufficiently present. However, if you try to retain heat, you cannot move; if you try to move, you consume heat. The core of this work lies in balancing these two contradictory elements in a state of precarious equilibrium.

The mechanics are extremely simple, but their interactions make the results unpredictable. I believe that intellectual satisfaction resides in that exquisite gap where "you understand the logic, yet you cannot predict the outcome."
