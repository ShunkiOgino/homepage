I have defined that "opinions and reflections cannot serve as anchors; only concrete facts—one's own actions and experiences—can." To automatically filter out posts that resemble current affairs commentary, I implemented a new "gate" to determine whether there are facts based on my own experience. It is a mechanism that automatically puts any material consisting solely of external events on hold.

When an AI lacks concreteness, the temptation is to add instructions like "be more specific" to the prompt. However, that is merely a temporary fix. The more instructions you add, the more prone the LLM becomes to contradictions, and ultimately, you end up relying on the "operational" cost of a human checking and correcting every single output.

Rather than fixing individual instances, I prioritize designing systems that can detect deficiencies in advance or structures that force the AI to operate correctly.

For example, to address the issue of the AI retreating into generalities, I installed a check gate based on "common density standards." I separated the removal of confidential information from abstraction and standardized the criteria for verbatim preservation of "valuable concretes," such as numerical data. By doing this, I eliminated redundant processing to shorten processing time while achieving output that maintains concreteness.

This approach is the process of replacing the operation of "individual checks" with a design of "deterministic wiring." The moment the AI retreats into generalities, it is rejected by the gate and automatically sent to "held" without ever reaching human eyes. I lay down a route in advance so that the correct answer is reached without requiring human effort.

That time you are spending right now fine-tuning prompts for AI output—could it be that you are trying to cover a flaw in the "wiring" through "operation"?
