When controlling AI characters through a retrospective mapping of "script → impression → emotion → motion," they were merely reacting to text; there was no sense that they were speaking of their own accord.

Initially, I followed a process where I first completed the script, then attached emotion labels to it, and finally assigned corresponding motions. However, this "after-the-fact" structure is precisely the source of that unnaturalness.

To address this, I am experimenting with a method where "gesture tags" are output simultaneously during the script generation stage. By forcing the AI to choose its acting with the same hand it uses to choose its words, I aimed for a synchronization of thought and action.

Humans determine their gestures and facial expressions at the exact moment they are choosing their words. Thought and action should be a single, inseparable process.

This is an approach designed so that the hand choosing the words and the hand choosing the gestures emerge from the same place, rather than applying a filter to the output after it's done. Instead of attaching emotions as retrospective labels, motions are integrated as part of the output known as language. Only by doing this does the image of a "character speaking with their own will" finally emerge.

When you give instructions to an AI, are you merely "correcting a finished product"? Or are you designing the process of thought itself?
