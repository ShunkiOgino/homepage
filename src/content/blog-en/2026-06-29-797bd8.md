How do you translate the absolute constraints of physical laws into a pleasant gaming experience? This experimental attempt resulted in 'game_orbit,' a physics game set in a world of simple dots.

At the core of this work is the management of a limited resource: "heat." The player cannot generate heat on their own; to compensate for the heat lost over time, they must collide with other dots and convert kinetic energy into heat. The controls are extremely simple—you can only launch yourself—but deep trade-offs lurk beneath the surface.

The first contradiction is "stasis versus movement." If dots huddle together, they stay warm, but if they cling too closely, they become unable to move. On the other hand, if you hold too much heat, you are blasted away. You must constantly oscillate between "warming up and stopping" and "moving," and this fluctuation provides the player with a pleasant sense of tension.

Furthermore, I set the condition for leveling up such that both "heat" and "momentum" must simultaneously exceed a certain threshold. Inherently, moving consumes heat and stopping loses momentum, meaning these two states are fundamentally difficult to reconcile. However, the experience of overlapping these two contradictory states for just a single moment is precisely where the pleasure of this game lies.

I also incorporated the concept of "metabolism" into the system. The cluster you bond with becomes a nucleus and grows, but maintaining a massive structure requires more heat. As a result, chilled individuals naturally detach. This creates a dynamic structure that is constantly renewing itself rather than one that simply expands infinitely.

Since all the other dots operate under these same rules, they are drawn toward warm areas without being told to do so, and structures form naturally. This is exactly like the cycle of the universe itself.

To take an abstract concept like the Second Law of Thermodynamics and translate it into a form that is "understood through touch" rather than explanation. To have the player experience the process of struggling within physical constraints while still trying to create a form—that was my intention for this work.
