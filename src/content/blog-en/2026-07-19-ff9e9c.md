Just how much can be inferred from observable quantitative data alone? I want to clarify where that boundary lies.

To explore this, I took an approach of making inferences based on the objective facts found in chat app message histories—specifically, "this was said on this date." I wanted a design grounded in fact, rather than declarative data like a calendar.

However, I hit a structural problem here. Because data like message histories is unstructured, applying an LLM (Large Language Model) becomes essential to extract meaning and connect it to an inference. This is the point where I feel, "The fact that an LLM is mandatory here is the bottleneck. Hmm, it's almost frightening."

I believe this is a question of epistemological limits. What can be derived from data is, at most, the realm of probability—the "be going to"—which exists as an extension of the present based on observable footprints. Subjective "will" or the realm of "perception" that only a living human possesses cannot be inferred, no matter how much data you accumulate. That area should remain a domain that can only be confirmed through direct contact and dialogue.

Inference via data, and interpretation via LLM. Within that dependency, what is the true nature of what we believe we have "understood"?
