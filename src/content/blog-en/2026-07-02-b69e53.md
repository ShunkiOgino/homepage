When I rewrite and save a personality definition in the admin panel, the character's behavior changes in about 5 seconds. No program restart is required.

When trying to give an AI a consistent personality, some people might feel that the correct answer is to rely on a spiritual approach—namely, "more sophisticated prompting." However, I took a structural approach instead, separating personality into "data" and "logic."

Previously, I managed personality definitions as fixed strings within the code, but this required a deployment every time I made an update, making the cost of experimentation too high. Specifically, what I did was migrate personality definitions to external data managed by a database. By doing this, the logic of "how to make it speak" was separated from the data of "who is speaking," which dramatically accelerated the behavior update cycle.

Additionally, to maintain consistency across multiple channels, I adopted an integrated model of "one person = one agent." I perform identity resolution via user IDs and dynamically inject a two-stage memory: "Counterparty Cards" containing attribute information for each person, and "Rolling Summaries" that aggregate the last six exchanges. This ensures that context is maintained regardless of which window is used for access.

What is crucial here is the control over the "amount" of injected information. Rather than using the maximum context length the model can handle, I established a budget based on the "effective attention span"—the range within which it can consistently perform complex processing. Based on the characteristics of Gemma-4, I set the upper limit for one track to approximately 3,000 tokens (roughly 2,000 to 3,000 Japanese characters) and introduced a mechanism that automatically splits or compresses data when this is exceeded. Because of this budget limit, the personality does not blur even as the amount of information increases; instead, it maintains a structure that preserves a sharp perspective.

The thinking behind this design is to transform personality from a "consistent will" into a computable "reproducibility of state." By turning personality definitions into a database and managing the amount of memory injection as a cost structure, I have eliminated individual dependency and reduced it to an operable system.

Perhaps individuality is nothing more than the arrangement of data.
