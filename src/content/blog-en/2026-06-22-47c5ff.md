Deviation is the proof of intent.

What determines human-likeness is not a "static attribute" like voice quality, but the "dynamic structure" of the time axis—in other words, the control of information volume through non-uniformity. Mechanical uniformity becomes noise to the listener and, as a result, significantly impairs expressiveness.

When specifically designing this non-uniformity, I place importance on optimizing three parameters: "intonation," "tempo dynamics," and "pauses." In practice, I found that most monotonous delivery was caused by all parameters being in a constant-speed state of 1.0. Simply varying these values, creating waves in the speed, and optimizing the pauses dramatically improves expressiveness without changing the voice quality itself.

In contrast, humans unconsciously warp the time axis when they speak. They slow down at critical points and place "pauses" in the gaps of their thoughts. This bias in the allocation of temporal cost is exactly what is conveyed to the listener as emotion or intent.

While an approach that pursues voice quality as a "stock" is important, I feel from experience that controlling the "flow" of the time axis is the shortest route to achieving human-likeness.

Deviation is the proof of intent.
