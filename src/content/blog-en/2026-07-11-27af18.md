A post-hoc mapping of script → impression → emotion → motion resulted in characters that felt unnatural, as if they were merely reacting to the text.

Initially, my process was to first create a script, then generate an impression like "this is a scene where they are angry," define the emotion, and finally assign a motion. However, characters produced through this structure don't seem to speak with their own will; they look as if they are simply "reacting to a completed sentence." This slight discrepancy was the true source of the unnaturalness felt by the viewer.

To resolve this discomfort, I am focusing on a fundamental change to the generation process.

First, for visual control, I separated the constantly playing idle motions from the emotion-based facial expressions, ensuring that the former does not overwrite or cancel out the latter. This raises the baseline for the character's "presence as a living being."

However, the essential solution is a method where "gesture tags" are output simultaneously during the script generation stage. For example, by embedding tags like [nod] or [shrug] within the text, I make the AI choose the acting for that moment with the same hand it uses to choose its words. By discarding the existing post-hoc pipeline and letting thought and action be determined in a single process, I am attempting to ensure consistency as an "act of speaking" rather than a reaction to text.

Choosing the acting at the same time and with the same hand as choosing the words. This synchronized design is what brings out expressive power.

Is it a matter of adding settings after the fact? Or is it about creating a structure where thought and behavior are born simultaneously?
