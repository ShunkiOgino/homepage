I design my information by dividing it into four layers: Personal, Confidential, Shareable upon request, and Public.

My perspective here isn't about simple scope control; it's about how to translate the "equivalence of semantic value and risk" into a concrete structure.

Normally, when making information public, one might think, "Let me erase the confidential information here." You identify the dangerous fields and strip them out. However, this method is always haunted by the possibility of human error—the "forgot to erase" mistake. You are entrusting your safety to the limits of your own attention.

I reverse that approach. Instead of filtering out the dangerous elements, I use only the fields permitted for disclosure to build a new dataset from scratch. This is my "structural gate."

Specifically, when generating data for public release, I don't shave unnecessary parts off the internal fixed data; instead, I extract only the permitted items to assemble a new dict (dictionary-format data). By doing this, I create a structure where facts that must remain internal are physically unlikely to leak in.

Furthermore, I've established a "one-way wall" in the information generation flow. The line for reading raw logs before approval and the pool of approved cards are completely separated. I strictly enforce a single rule: "Output only from approved information," ensuring that unapproved information is never touched in subsequent processes. In this way, the risk of unintentional exposure is blocked by structure rather than individual caution.

Through this design, I've come to realize that every time value is created by sharing a piece of information, an equal weight of risk is placed on the other side of the scale. Semantic value and the risk of exposure are essentially equivalent.

That is why it is crucial to separate by structure rather than simply increasing the precision of management. In particular, for the middle layer of information—that which is "shareable upon request"—the fact that uninterested people are naturally filtered out becomes a filter in itself, resulting in the value of the information reaching only the appropriate party.

Right now, what am I erasing, and what am I selecting?
