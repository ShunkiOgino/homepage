"Character remains still for the 0.45 seconds while the card flies; gesture begins exactly upon landing." By implementing this second-by-second motion synchronization, I resolved the misalignment of visual guidance in AI video generation.

You might think it would be more efficient to let the AI handle the optimization. However, increasing the level of abstraction simultaneously increases the areas that are beyond your control. I prioritized the opposite: decomposing processes into primitives (the smallest possible units) and controlling each behavior deterministically.

I struggled particularly with controlling the ambiguous realm of visual "comfort." Even when giving instructions to the AI, the timing between the direction and the movement was slightly off, creating moments where the viewer wasn't sure where to look. To eliminate this dissonance, I measured the time that triggers the effect and integrated a process to freeze the character for a fixed value of "0.45 seconds." By clearly defining the causal relationship—that motion begins the moment of landing—I eliminated the AI's subjective interpretation and realized a structure that outputs consistent quality every time.

Rather than relying on high-level abstraction, I decompose processes to their smallest units and provide a clear correct answer for each. I believe that the accumulation of this procedure is, in the end, the shortest route to constructing the most efficient and robust system.

That instability is a sign of an abstraction that has not been fully decomposed.
