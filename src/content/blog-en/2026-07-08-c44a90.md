"Character remains still for the 0.45 seconds the card is flying; gestures begin the moment it lands." By implementing this second-by-second motion synchronization, I resolved the misalignment of gaze guidance in AI video generation.

You might think it's more efficient to let the AI handle optimization. However, increasing the level of abstraction simultaneously increases the areas that are beyond your control. I prioritized the opposite: decomposing processes into primitives (the smallest possible units) and controlling each behavior deterministically.

I struggled particularly with the ambiguous realm of visual "comfort." Even when giving instructions to the AI, the timing between the direction and the movement was slightly off, creating moments where the viewer wasn't sure where to look. To eliminate this dissonance, I measured the exact time that triggers the effect and incorporated a process to keep the character still for a fixed value of "0.45 seconds." By clearly defining the causal relationship—that motion begins exactly upon landing—I eliminated the AI's subjective interpretation and realized a structure that outputs consistent quality every time.

Rather than relying on high-level abstraction, I decompose processes to their smallest units and provide a clear "correct answer" for each. I believe that accumulating these procedures is, in the end, the shortest route to building the most efficient and robust system.

That instability is a sign of an abstraction that hasn't been fully decomposed.
