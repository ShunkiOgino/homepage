There is a case where, when building a 100,000-GPU class cluster, the years-long wait for power grid connection was bypassed by hauling gas turbines on-site to generate their own power, achieving operational status in just a few months.

When it comes to infrastructure construction, the "correct answer" is often thought to be waiting for the external environment to be prepared and ensuring alignment. It was generally accepted that physical constraints, such as the development of power grids, are irreversible, and that planning within those schedules was the only way to minimize risk.

However, the action taken by xAI's Colossus was to rewrite that very premise. By not relying on existing infrastructure like the power grid and instead bringing in their own power generation equipment, they physically erased the biggest bottleneck: the wait for connection. What was crucial here was not capital power or technical optimization, but rather the execution power—the speed of physically getting it built.

Infrastructure hegemony in the AI era is no longer decided by the skill of design alone. No matter how superior your proposal for computing resource configuration is, if it isn't physically operational, its value is zero. The ability to break through existing constraints, such as waiting for grid connection, by introducing alternative means and drastically shortening the time to operation—this "physical completion speed" is what becomes a decisive competitive advantage.

Structurally speaking, this means converting "waiting time" into "capital expenditure cost." Time cannot be recovered, but money and physical means (gas turbines) can be procured. By solving an irreversible timeline with reversible costs, they bought back time, resulting in the overwhelming speed difference of becoming operational in a matter of months.

Perhaps what is "impossible" isn't the physical constraint itself, but simply being bound by procedure.
