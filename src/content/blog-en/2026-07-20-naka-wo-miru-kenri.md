The world is divided into the consumers and the producers, with a wall standing between them. From the buyer's side, you can see the price and the packaging, but you cannot see how it is made, who decides what, or where the money flows. Employment is nearly the only publicly open gate that allows you to cross this wall.

In other words, while employment looks like a "scenario where you sell your labor," it actually contains a simultaneous reverse transaction. You are paying with your labor as an admission fee to buy a ticket into the world of those who create.
The reason job postings don't describe the inner workings isn't out of malice; it's because there is no way to describe them from the outside.

And the only thing that remains from this purchase is neither the salary nor the job title, but the understanding that settles in your mind. However, learning has a saturation point. What you can learn by observing increases rapidly in the first one to two years, after which it becomes a repetition of the same scenery. Therefore, the rational approach is a time-limited apprenticeship: once you have learned everything there is to learn, you move on.

I am not saying don't get a job. I am saying that it is a purchase. Buy it after seeing what's inside. You are free to decide the price and the deadline yourself.
