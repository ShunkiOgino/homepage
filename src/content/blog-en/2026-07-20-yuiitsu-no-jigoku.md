Giving up is a correct answer. Lower your desires to the floor and live an easy life. No one can blame you for that.

Putting in the work is also a correct answer. Shoulder the gear necessary to match your ambitions and move to the side of those who create. Both paths are perfectly valid.

The only ones who get punished are those in the middle: those who keep their desires on the side of "wanting change," yet continue to spend their days without building anything. They wait for a promised ladder. They buy lottery tickets. They say "someday." The only difference between prayer and design is whether or not you are moving the variables that your own hands can control.

Those who quit were correct; those who settled in were correct. The only thing that is truly painful is spending the same day over and over while thinking, "I hate it like this."

The cost of remaining miserable is a monthly subscription; the cost of deciding is a one-time payment. The true identity of that pain isn't the work itself, but rather the state of having not yet decided.

If you feel that you are working hard but aren't being rewarded, before you increase the amount of effort, first check if the place where you are working is actually connected to the plumbing of what you want. No matter how much you pour into a pipe that isn't connected, it will never reach the destination.

You don't have to decide today. Simply admitting that you "haven't decided yet" will make it clear what you need to research next. Every choice is permitted. The only things that are punished are the failure to choose, and the discrepancy between desire and action.
