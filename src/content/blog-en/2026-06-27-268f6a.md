Protecting through structure is not the same as giving up on trust.

When we "trust" someone, or a system like AI, we are unconsciously relying on uncertain factors: the other party's will, their ethics, or the accuracy of a learning model. From a structural perspective, however, this is an extremely high-risk design. This is because will and ethics fluctuate depending on the situation, and model accuracy is vulnerable to unknown inputs (edge cases).

I believe that the key to establishing autonomous delegation of authority lies not in "trust," but in "structural guardrails." In other words, it is an approach that ensures safety by creating a state of physical impossibility rather than relying on goodwill.

### Implementing "Impossibility" in AI

When granting authority to AI workers, I prioritize OS-level isolation and the physical blocking of access rights over constraints imposed by prompts.

Specifically, I have built a design where the AI is structurally unable to reach confidential information by applying sandboxing to arbitrary code execution tools and introducing "write scopes" based on roles. In public channels, I block the injection of private memories and insert a safety filter immediately before output; this ensures that even if there is contaminated input from the outside, a total system collapse or information leak cannot occur.

### Balancing Runaway Prevention via Cost Limits and Autonomy

At this point, the question may arise: "Won't imposing limits impair autonomy?" In reality, it is the opposite.

To achieve "approval-less" operation—eliminating the need for human approval every time—I introduced a physical cost barrier in the form of rate limits. By setting a total daily reply count and a limit on replies to the same recipient (e.g., 20 total / 3 per person), I can confine the scope of impact to a limited numerical value even if the AI enters a loop or runs amok. By controlling via physical quantities—resources—rather than relying on will, humans can ultimately delegate authority with peace of mind.

### True Autonomy Brought by Structure

It is precisely because there are robust guardrails that say "you absolutely cannot go beyond this point" that we can confidently delegate authority and the AI can operate autonomously.

In the end, this is a conversation about trust.
