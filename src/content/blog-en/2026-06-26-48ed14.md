Telling an AI "don't handle confidential information" is not a structural solution. The more constraints you add, the further you drift from actual management. Why?

Because I believe that trust is not about "asking someone to behave correctly," but about "creating a structure where incorrect behavior is physically impossible."

When we trust someone or something and delegate authority, we tend to rely on uncertain things like the "integrity of the partner" or the "precision of the system." Thinking that "they should follow the rules" or "it should be fine because we did enough testing" seems rational on the surface, but it is essentially a precarious state that depends on another's will or probability.

### Implementing "Physical Isolation" in AI Workers

In the authority design of autonomously operating AI workers, the important thing is to create regions that are physically unreachable.

Specifically, I implement "write scopes" that restrict write destinations to specific directories and use OS-level sandboxing for code execution tools to block access. Furthermore, to achieve "approval-less" operation—removing the need for human approval every time—I establish guardrails such as rate limits on the total number of replies per day or the number of replies to a single recipient. For example, if continuous replies to the same user in a short period are not physically blocked, it is impossible to stop an AI from spiraling into spamming.

Additionally, in public channels, I structurally block the injection of private memories and pass outputs through a safety filter immediately before they are sent. This ensures that even if there is contaminated input from the outside, it cannot reach internal data.

### Conclusion: The Freedom Brought by Impossibility

Management that relies on "will" or "ethics" always comes with the cost of surveillance. However, if trust is guaranteed through "structural impossibility," such as physical isolation of authority and resource limits, management costs are minimized.

It is precisely because there are constraints that we can let it move freely. That is the entirety of trust.
